Welcome to the demo website of the Indian Cancer Database for Translational Research- ACTREC (ICDTRA)!

Use the menu above to navigate between sections.

This is a demo website meant to demonstrate some of the functionality intended for the final ICDTRA website. Currently, the Cancer Genomics section offers a limited selection of differential expression data from the genomics expression database EMBL-EBI Expression Atlas (https://www.ebi.ac.uk/gxa/home), within the “Differential Gene Expression” sub-section, which also offers lists of the names of proteins/genes associated with signalling pathways, as sourced from the Reactome database’s Pathway Browser (https://reactome.org/PathwayBrowser/). The “Gene Biomarker Data From Literature” sub-section lists data on biomarkers gathered from the literature on cancer. The Cancer Literature section currently allows users to search through a set of research articles featuring “cancer” and “differential expression” and “India” in the articles. The list of publications can be viewed, and the articles can be downloaded as PDF files, in cases where the articles are open-source. The Cancer Proteomics section will operate similarly to the Cancer Genomics section, although the “differential protein expression with biological fold-change from a proteomics database” part of this section is not active, at present, owing to difficulties in obtaining freely available differential expression data, organised in a database, through automation. The Diagnostics section presently displays biomarker data drawn from the literature on cancer, by Google’s Notebook LM (https://notebooklm.google/). The corresponding sub-sections in the Cancer Genomics and Cancer Proteomics sections are derived from the table in this section. The Diagnostics section also mentions approaches that could be taken for providing information on diagnostics tools/techniques and biorepositories. The latter is useful for promoting research collaboration of cancer biorepositories with basic science cancer researchers. The Treatment Options section presently displays a list of National Cancer Grid (NCG) member hospitals/institutes listed by the Tata Memorial Centre (TMC) (https://www.ncgindia.org/). It also lists approaches to providing information to the user on drugs available in the Indian market. The Clinical Trials section lists approaches to informing the user about guidelines for the selection of patients as study subjects, drug-response prediction, druggable protein targets, and drugs under clinical trials. The Education section states that the cancer biology sub-section will be developed by referring to cancer biology review articles and taking input from the faculty at TMC-ACTREC. The Education section also houses sub-sections on high-throughput cancer data and cancer awareness programmes in India, providing relevant resources for both sub-sections.

This section currently contains research articles sourced from PubMed (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/) that were obtained through a search using the following search key, and then downloading all automatically downloadable articles from the search results: "cancer" + "differential expression" + "India". The research articles are sorted by year of publication, and the corresponding PDFs can be downloaded.

The differential protein and gene expression data derived (via the prompts shown below) from 50 articles in this section can be viewed in the Cancer Proteomics and Cancer Genomics sections.

After uploading 50 of these research article PDF files, for articles from 1996–2017, to Google’s Notebook LM AI (https://notebooklm.google.com/), the following prompt was posed to Notebook LM:

Assume that you are a cancer researcher with 20+ years of experience, especially in the fields of differential gene and protein expression. Please read the entire contents of all 50 research articles uploaded here and filter the articles to identify those that explicitly mention differential gene/protein expression in a cancer vs normal or cancer vs paratumoral or test-condition for cancer vs control-condition for cancer context. Next, on these filtered results, apply another filter to identify those research papers in which the fold change or biological fold change is reported either in result tables in the papers or somewhere in the main text. Tabulate these fold changes (which may have been reported in their source research papers directly or as a logarithm of the fold change) in one column, followed by the type of fold change reported (direct, log to the base 2, log to the base 10, etc…) in the next column, the citation of the research paper from which this value was sourced (as reported in the research paper in question) in the next column (for searching for the citation, use the full citation reported in the bibliography for the fold change value being reported in your result table, citing the full citation for the research paper for which PDF file has been named, in cases in which the fold change value is a research finding being reported as a part of the results of the research paper after which the PDF file has been named), followed by the type of cancer for which the fold change was reported, followed by the category of comparison (e.g. tumor/cancer vs normal, tumor/cancer vs paratumoral, tumor/cancer with test condition (e.g.: drug being tested) vs tumor/cancer with control/vector control, etc…). Restrict the set of fold change values reported in the result tables such that you report only those fold change values that pertain to fold changes in the expression of human (Homo sapiens) proteins or genes. Report data for fold change values found in these research papers only if they are definitely present in them. Please do not impute/hallucinate any data in the table. I would rather have no results in the table than have fabricated/false/hallucinated results in it. Thanks! If you have searched for both differential protein expression as well as differential gene expression in the first filter applied, then please split the results table into two results tables— one for each of these two categories.

In each result table, please add an Experiment column at the immediate right side of the one reporting fold change type. Populate it with the type of experiment used to obtain the fold change data (e.g.: mRNA sequencing, microarray, etc…) as sourced from the respective source research paper in which the fold change value in question was reported.

Please add columns titled Protein Name and Gene Name, to the immediate left of the column reporting the fold change value. Populate these columns, in each row below the title/header row, with the corresponding protein name and gene name, respectively, associated with the protein/gene for which the expression fold change value was reported, in the PDF file (research paper) from which the value was sourced. If either the protein name or the gene name associated with the protein/gene is not present in the respective research article, then place a dash (-) as the value in the corresponding cell in the table. Keep the column titles/headers of the two result tables the same across these result tables. Please add columns titled “Adjusted p-value”, “Raw p-value”, and “t-Statistic” immediately to the right of the Fold Change Type column. Populate these columns, below the header/title rows, with the corresponding adjusted p-value, raw p-value, and t-statistic for the fold change value reported in each row of the columns, respectively, sourcing these values from the corresponding research paper (PDF file) from which the respective fold change value was sourced. If the adjusted p-value, raw p-value, or t-statistic value to be filled in a cell in the result tables is not present in the corresponding research paper (PDF file), then fill the corresponding cell with a dash (-) as its cell value.

This generated two tables— one for differential gene expression data and another for differential protein expression data— both of which had their data sourced from the uploaded PDF files. The following addendum was then added as a follow-up prompt, to ensure that Notebook LM did not infer any of the data, but rather quoted it from the source PDF file directly in the tables:

If you haven’t already done so, while forming the final result tables reported in plain tabular form right here in the chat window, please ensure that search hits were only exact matches to all key phrases searched for during filtering and that all phrases reported in the content inside the cells of the tables were directly quoted from the respective source PDF files (research articles) and not inferred or paraphrased. Please display the result tables in the chat window above, after ensuring that they conform to these stipulations.

Diagnostic Tools

This sub-section will host information on diagnostic tools employed by clinicians to detect and diagnose the presence and prognosis of cancer, such as anatomic imaging techniques like computed tomography (CT) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), functional imaging techniques like positron emission tomography (PET) scanning, histopathology-based techniques like immunohistochemistry (IHC) and haematoxylin and eosin (H&E) staining, genomics-based techniques like next-generation sequencing (NGS) for high-throughput screening of genomes to look for mutations in DNA (genes) that could inform treatment decisions, and immunoassays like enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) used to detect concentrations of circulating protein biomarkers of cancer (e.g.: prostate specific antigen(PSA) for prostate cancer). This information will be collated from review articles and educational websites and will be published here, after being ratified by clinicians.

Biorepositories

TMC-ACTREC’s biorepository presently has ≤ 100 cryopreserved tumour samples, spanning a variety of cancer types, that were collected from multiple recurrences of solid cancerous tumours resected from the same patients. The paired clinical notes and treatment regimens applied, as accessed from the corresponding electronic medical records (EMRs) of these patients, are available to the biorepository. The collection of such tumour samples that were collected over the course of multiple recurrences could be used by researchers to identify changes in the concentrations of various transcripts (RNA) and proteins, for correlation with clinical characteristics and clinical endpoints such as survival or death, at the completion of a prescribed duration since the collection of the first sample, pending approval from the institutional review board (IRB). The EMR data paired with samples from a single patient could be made available to researchers after anonymisation. Listing such available sets of tumour samples collected from multiple recurrences that occurred in the same patient, along with the paired EMR after anonymisation, in this sub-section could facilitate research. Similar datasets could be listed from other cancer biorepositories in the country (e.g. the biorepository at Tata Memorial Hospital (TMH), Parel), which could greatly augment the usefulness of this part of the database.

Drug Response Prediction

This sub-section will encourage the users to search for details about the drug of choice in Drugbank’s web server (https://go.drugbank.com/), download the SMILES format designation for this drug, and enter this SMILES designation in the appropriate search field in the web server of SwissADME (https://www.swissadme.ch/). (ADME stands for Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, and Excretion.) SwissADME will then provide predictions of pharmacokinetic parameters- predictions of presence/extent or absence of absorption by the gastrointestinal tract, passage through the blood-brain barrier, passage through the P-glycoprotein drug efflux pump as a substrate, skin permeation, and inhibition of Cytochrome P450, in the human body. Searching with the SMILES designation in the ADMET prediction web-server ADMET-AI (https://admet.ai.greenstonebio.com/) will yield predictions of all these parameters of pharmacokinetics (PK), plus more PK parameters, and toxicity predictions (the ‘T’ in ‘ADMET’).

Druggable Targets- Signalling and Biochemical Pathways

The user will be asked to enter the SMILES designation of the drug of choice in the search box of the web server of SwissTarget (https://www.swisstargetprediction.ch/) and run a search. SwissTarget will then provide predictions of potential target proteins to which the submitted drug molecule might bind, along with probabilities of these binding interactions. Checking the UniProt IDs listed by SwissTarget, for the predicted target molecules, in the Reactome database (https://reactome.org/) will inform the user of the signalling pathways in which the potential target operates. Searching for the SMILES ID in DrugBank also provides the known mechanism of action of the drug, if any. The user could also enter the name of the drug or target to be queried into the search bar in the STITCH database web server (https://stitch-db.org/) and run a search, after selecting the organism. STITCH then reports a network of interactors for the queried item- targets for the queried drug or drugs known to interact with the queried target protein. STITCH thus provides the user with network pharmacology information.

Drugs Under Clinical Trials

The user could be directed to the US government’s official website for reporting clinical trials’ data (https://clinicaltrials.gov/), where the user could search for the type of cancer and the drugs used in the trial and select an appropriate trial from the search hits. One could discover either a drug used in a clinical trial for the treatment of the cancer type of interest or such a trial conducted using a particular combination of drugs.
